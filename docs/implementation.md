# Implementation decisions and remaining acceptance work

Date: 2026-10-30. Requirements remain in `requirements.md`.

## Architecture and precise semantics

Installable Electron desktop application with a private local origin, isolated preload bridge and a bundled C++ solver. An optional local HTTP preview is for development. A Web Worker isolates validation and independent EV evaluation from interaction. A pure JavaScript regret-matching implementation remains an independent bounded-game reference. Source and diagnostics are reproducible. No GPU dependency.

### Full-hand mode

Hero can be OOP or IP and acts on every applicable street. Logical player 0 remains hero and player 1 remains opponent; physical position separately controls the first actor and native range/player mapping. Missing position in older scenarios defaults to OOP. Both players can check, bet, call, fold and raise within the configured per-street menus, including all-ins. The native tree has a limit of three raises after an initial bet. The converter derives contributions, call gaps, minimum raises, terminal states and street resets independently. Every configured size and every compatible chance child must exist; only rule-derived terminal children may be absent from the native export. Illegal private/public card collisions are excluded.

Best-response evaluation propagates counterfactual opponent reach, sums hidden hands before choosing an action, and conditions chance probabilities on both private hands. Initial-pot EVs sum to the initial pot. The zero-rake Nash gap is the sum of the two best-response values minus the initial pot. Hero decision EVs use optimal future continuation against the fixed opponent model. Impossible opponent-model histories display no model reach. Individual scores compare all offered actions, including sizes with zero target frequency. Full-hand frequency groups separate public action history, board, private hand, exact scenario and sampling target.

Animated training traverses only the saved tree, pauses on each hero decision and keeps opponent cards and EV feedback hidden until completion. Check-through hands still pause on turn and river. All-in runouts animate without requesting unavailable actions; folds do not reveal unused cards. Per-decision review and public-action replay are recorded in the journal. Large complete trees use IndexedDB and restore without recomputation. Portable full-hand solution packages contain only scenario settings and baseline policies; import reconstructs legal states, checks all configured branches, and recalculates every decision EV and the independent gap without running the native solver. Scenario-only exports still require precomputation.

Full-hand resource guards: twelve exact combos per player, one or two seed bet and raise percentages per street, pot >=4 bb, stack >=1 bb, SPR <=3, zero rake, 600,000 validated nodes, 2,400,000 baseline probability values and 256 MB native export. Deep can expand to four bet and three raise percentages per street, before legal all-ins; the same tree/export guards apply. Native solving has a 30-minute limit and up to four threads. Requested native accuracy corresponds to a 0.001 bb Nash gap, but the independently measured final gap determines the displayed precision status. A completed tree can be nonconverged. No global action-restriction or robustness bound is claimed.

Full-tree regression verifies native/independent gap agreement, all three streets with raises, rejection of missing legal branches, solution export/import round-trip equivalence, and 100 simulated hands using the re-imported solution. Package tests also reject missing river branches, altered size settings and invalid policy distributions, and prove that injected EV/gap/terminal-state fields cannot alter the independently reconstructed result. A two-bet-per-street 1v1 fixture produced 378,438 nodes and a 0.00005836 bb gap. The 3x3 default-range diagnostic at 500 iterations took 255.9 seconds with one thread and produced a 0.050765 bb gap; it did not meet precision. These measurements are different inputs and are not an efficiency comparison.

### Legacy bounded mode

Version 0.1 addresses a bounded scenario family, not arbitrary no-limit play. Hero is OOP at a fixed flop. Aggression is either an all-in or one of the configured/selected bet sizes. Checking commits both players to a check-down. A call commits both players to a check-down; folds are terminal. Thus all choices exposed during training have complete saved equity coverage; there are no unsolved turn/river actions disguised as ready.

Chance sampling: probability of a compatible private-hand pair is proportional to the product of its two configured weights. Overlaps are excluded and remaining pairs normalized jointly. Runouts are uniform without replacement conditional on both private hands. Exact equity enumeration averages all 990 unordered runouts. Training samples ordered turn/river cards; these induce the same equity distribution.

EV convention: measured from the current decision, with the existing pot sunk. Checking pays equity times the pot after rake. Called shove pays equity times the final pot after rake, minus the new contribution. Fold wins the current pot after rake. Ties receive half the pot. Rake is taken from all terminal pots, including folds, with a zero cap meaning zero rake.

Opponent model: baseline call probability c is mapped to c+(1-c)t for t>=0, and c(1+t) for t<0. Per-combination overrides take precedence. No adaptation occurs after fixing this model. Deviation d blends baseline hero behavior with an exact best response, (1-d)baseline+d response. This has no certified robustness bound. Equally valued actions retain baseline mixing.

Reference precision: configurable 10k/50k/100k regret iterations. Native budget capped at 10k iterations and 0.001% requested accuracy. Measured final gap, not iteration count or native success exit, is the quality diagnostic. With rake the sum of unilateral incentives is a diagnostic at the returned profile, without a zero-sum convergence guarantee. Arithmetic is floating point; there is no card sampling error. Action restriction loss is unknown.

Resource bound: 12 exact combinations per player, at most 144 equity entries / 142,560 evaluated runouts. Result matrix memory below 1 MB excludes browser, evaluator temporaries, native lookup tables and process overhead. This is a deliberate capacity limit, not an 8 GB hardware benchmark.

## Sizing contract

Candidates are capped at stack and rounded to 0.001 bb; deep inserts adjacent midpoints. Search is finite (at most nine bets). Exhaustive subset selection measures range-weighted best-response loss versus the complete evaluated candidate set and the same fixed opponent model. No global sizing bound or restricted-tree equilibrium claim is made. Baseline is the candidate-tree solution; retained hero frequencies are renormalized before deviation blending. Numerical convergence (0.001 bb displayed target), candidate simplification loss, and unknown loss versus untested legal sizes are distinct. Native amounts are scaled by 1000 to avoid whole-chip truncation; automatic all-in substitution is disabled, and returned sizes must match exactly.

## Analysis and review semantics

Equity and composition use private-hand marginal probabilities from the compatible joint deal distribution. At a saved full-hand node, actual reach includes both players' prior policies and exact public cards, separately selectable for baseline/baseline or adjusted-hero/fixed-opponent profiles. This differs from counterfactual opponent reach used for decision EV. Joint weights are normalized after each step and the absolute history probability is accumulated in log space; an underflowing absolute probability does not discard conditional weights. Truly zero-probability histories have undefined conditional ranges and equity, with no fallback to starting ranges. Chance probabilities use 45 possible turn cards and 44 river cards per compatible private-hand pair. Public-card conflicts are shown with zero mass and no equity or hand classification.

Blocker percentage is the opposing board-compatible, action-weighted range removed by the selected private hand before private-card collision removal; it is descriptive card removal, not an EV causal attribution. Straight and flush patterns use the currently known cards; raw straight outs are not corrected for hidden cards and do not imply winning outs. River hands have no unfinished draws. Hands without compatible deals show no equity. Weighted 13×13 cells sum exact-combination conditional masses. Exact equity averages remaining legal runouts before future betting, including at fold nodes; it is not the fold payoff. Reports are computed lazily and reuse a per-explorer equity cache. Targeting conditions the same weighted pair distribution on the chosen group. Mixed-frequency statistics group the exact scenario configuration, private hand, action amounts, and sampling target separately; target probabilities are averaged over recorded decisions. Individual action scores remain EV loss. Replay steps through flop, decision, and terminal reveal; folds do not reveal unused runout cards in newly recorded hands.

Detailed opponent edits use semantic action/card paths and a tree-identity signature. Range order is included because policy rows are indexed. Every edit must name an opponent decision, a compatible private combination and every legal action with a normalized distribution. Up to 64 edited decisions and 50 KB are allowed. Edited distributions take precedence over call tendencies; baseline convergence is unchanged. Changes to model/deviation/menu limits reuse the saved baseline only when tree identity and iteration budget match. Worker cancellation or failure preserves the previous scenario. Portable imports revalidate edits and recompute feedback.

## Full-hand tendency contract

`fullTendencies` contains optional `bluff`, `raise` and `classes` fields. Each tendency is finite and in [−1,1]. Class keys 0–8 follow high card through straight flush; each class may override `call`, `bet` and `raise`. A blank UI field inherits the global setting; explicit zero overrides it with a neutral adjustment. Classification uses the best current five-card hand, including the board, on every street. High-card aggression (both betting and raising) is a limited bluff proxy including draws. The made-hand global control changes raising only. Paired boards and weak made hands are not classified by equity. These are fixed-model assumptions, not inferred opponent facts.

For a legal action group's current mass p, tendency t maps it to p+(1−p)t for t≥0, otherwise p(1+t). Relative probabilities within the group and its complement are preserved; a zero-mass group splits new mass uniformly among its legal members. First apply the class/global aggression adjustment, then the class/global calling adjustment. Exact-combination absolute call probabilities override the calling target. Consequently later adjustments can change earlier group totals, including reintroducing probability when the complement previously had zero mass. A detailed per-node/per-combination lock replaces the entire row and wins over every tendency. Unspecified settings preserve the existing model. Baseline policies and gap remain unchanged; response EVs, menus and training feedback are reevaluated from the saved baseline.

Exports containing `fullTendencies` use schema 4 for both scenarios and complete solutions, so older readers reject them. Formats 1–3 remain readable. Settings are also preserved in local library saves and exact-configuration training statistics. Strict import validation rejects unknown keys, unsupported classes and invalid values.

Verification: all 59 local tests, syntax checks and isolated desktop/native/package-worker smoke pass. Tests cover all-street high-card adjustments, paired-board classification, class/combination/node precedence, zero/tiny probability groups, model identity and package round trips. `node native/verify-tendencies.mjs` reused a 229,446-node native fixture, checked 48,052 opponent policy rows, round-tripped schema 4 and completed 100 hands (181 choices), with zero new native solves. ## Turn and river card comparison

`compareNextCards` starts at one saved chance decision. It validates the union of structurally legal cards against the configured private pairs and current board before reporting complete next-card coverage. Conditional card probability is the sum of incoming joint pair weights for pairs that do not hold that card, divided by 45 before the turn or 44 before the river. Incoming weights follow both players' earlier actions under either baseline/baseline or adjusted-hero/fixed-opponent play. It does not divide absolute reach probabilities, so an underflowing absolute history probability can still have valid conditional weights.

For each positive-probability card, the report reuses conditional node analysis for exact remaining-runout equity, made hands, draws and the first acting player's frequencies. Weighted totals multiply these quantities by conditional card probabilities; action groups include exact kinds and amounts. Equity is before subsequent betting, not decision EV. Both ranges' equity totals are complementary. A missing card, invalid board transition or missing reachable strategy rejects the report; it does not renormalize a partial sample into an exhaustive claim. Structurally legal but zero-profile cards remain listed with zero probability and undefined conditional summaries. Entirely zero-reach histories have no probability distribution or averages.

The English/Chinese explorer panel is lazy, shares its profile with node-range analysis, sorts by card/equity/betting frequency, and links each card to its saved opening decision. A separate expandable table compares both ranges' hand strength for every card. Tiny positive percentages display `<0.01%` rather than zero. These reports compare the next cards at one exact betting history, including any chosen turn's river distribution; they do not silently pool different histories, opponent models or multiple flops. Saved-flop comparisons are a separate report described below; cross-history aggregation remains pending.

## Selected saved-flop comparison

`flop-report.js` compares the opening nodes of two or more complete local-library solutions. Equal weights give each selected flop equal mass; custom positive weights are normalized within each compatible group. Neither is an implied natural flop-deal distribution. The report is a selected sample, not exhaustive flop coverage. Exact ranges are sorted and their weights normalized for grouping; proportional representations of the same distribution match. Name, flop and iteration budget are excluded from group identity. Position, pot/stack, rake, candidate menus, sizing settings, calling/hand-class tendencies, overrides, deviation and detailed opponent-edit basis remain part of identity. Different models never get one pooled average. Board-bound detailed edits stay separate conservatively.

Each row retains its flop, sample share, both equities and made-hand/draw composition, opening action frequencies, iteration budget and independent baseline gap. Group summaries use the declared sample weights. Opening frequencies follow the selected baseline or adjusted/fixed-opponent profile; best-response EV always uses optimal continuation against the configured fixed opponent and is not the selected mixed strategy's payoff. Baseline gap, incomplete convergence and sizing limitations remain distinct. Duplicate unordered flops within a compatible group are rejected, so two saves of the same flop do not double its sample mass.

`flop-report-worker.js` loads IndexedDB trees sequentially and retains only small summaries. It reads results already validated by solving/import, checks readiness, exact saved-scenario identity, opening node and strategy, and finite quality metrics. Missing or stale entries fail the whole comparison instead of silently reducing coverage. Cancellation terminates the worker. The report never loads a selected scenario into the active training state. Selection, weights, profile and completed report survive navigation and language changes; saving changed library data invalidates the report. The panel also works when the active scenario has no solution.

## Preflop range construction and provenance

The canonical form sorts exact combinations; overlaps and malformed weights fail. Zero-weight entries are excluded after duplicate detection. The matrix uses 6/4/12-combination class denominators and preserves distinct suit weights. These inclusion weights are not normalized marginal deal probabilities. The standalone range parser does not change postflop solver limits.

Range documents whitelist a name, canonical current/original weights, table/position/stack/history/bet-size/rake/ante context and source/citation/terms/quality notes. Unknown assumptions remain blank, never inferred. They contain no training records. Edits preserve the original range and are labeled as a user study model. Local storage supports 100 named documents; quota or malformed-library failures do not overwrite existing storage. Imports are limited to 1 MiB. Four original mathematical hand-family templates are not positional strategy charts or equilibrium recommendations. Applying a range removes only combinations containing configured flop cards and keeps all other weights unchanged. ## Resource preflight

`full-resources.js` counts the configured postflop tree without building every public-board branch. Betting transitions depend on street length, contributions, position and sizes, not card identities. A memoized traversal counts per-street nodes and per-actor action slots; a separate enumeration computes the union of compatible public boards and legal actor-row counts. Multiplying these counts gives the same nodes and baseline probability slots as full expansion. All-in/fold leaves remain terminal and do not invent extra runout nodes. The memoization cache has a 100,000-state guard. The native adapter repeats both node and probability capacity checks before binary availability checks, temporary files or process creation; the renderer checks before changing the current solution. Import conversion also counts probability values before allocating each decision's policy rows.

The report includes the expanded Deep candidates, even when only one bet/raise size will be retained. Iteration budget and opponent/deviation changes do not change tree size. The compact payload is defined as eight bytes per baseline probability; it excludes objects, native state, temporary arrays, extra policies/EVs, export buffers, UI and operating-system memory. It is not a peak-RAM estimate or an 8 GB qualification. Such hardware measurements remain required.

## Durable native progress

It saves regrets, cached regret sums, accumulated strategy and absolute iteration/convergence state for every created public-runout slot. Checkpoints stream to disk rather than materializing a second JSON tree. The temporary file is flushed and atomically replaced; a checksum, expected dimensions, finite-value checks and complete game/tree metadata protect loading. A SHA-256 identity binds the exact executable and commands except the iteration budget. Different game settings or engine builds cannot silently reuse state. Abrupt closure can lose work since the most recent completed save. A completed native baseline reloads without further iterations. Full-tree conversion and EV evaluation restart if interrupted; they are not yet internally checkpointed. Local checkpoint files are distinct from validated portable solution packages and contain no training journal. ## Compact card evaluation and expanded study ranges

`full-cards.js` shares exact card information across betting histories with the same flop. Conversion caches compatible private-pair counts and actor sets by public runout; the original pair-index lists were replaced by the blocker-count optimization described below. River comparison uses the two cached ranks directly. Turn/river order shares the same showdown rank and card-removal set, while chance traversal still preserves ordered deal probabilities. The existing seven-card evaluator and full enumeration remain unchanged. Equity tables allocate only when equity is requested; conversion alone does not allocate them. ## Blocker-count coverage and broad-range feasibility

Coverage no longer retains a list of private-hand pairs for each board. After excluding public-card collisions, `rangeBoardCoverage` counts legal opponent hands for each private hand by inclusion-exclusion: subtract hands holding either private card, then add back the identical two-card hand once. A hand has a policy row exactly when at least one compatible opponent hand remains. This computes the same pair count and actor sets in time proportional to the sum of range lengths, with no per-board pair list. It assumes the complete Cartesian pairing of two unique, positive-weight ranges; it is not an action-conditioned reach calculator. Existing equity evaluation still uses joint pair reach.

Preflight uses the same exact blocker counts and computes unordered turn/river coverage once, multiplying by two to preserve ordered-board totals. Betting traversal, chance probabilities, actions, strategy values, range limits and resource caps are unchanged. Tests independently enumerate pairs across every legal river board for overlapping ranges, cover identical/dead hands, and check closed-form counts for all 1,326 starting combinations.
